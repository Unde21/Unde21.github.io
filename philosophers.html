The problem

      
Philosophers sit around a table and need two forks to eat. Nobody may go more than time_to_die ms without a meal. Mandatory part: one thread per philosopher and one mutex per fork, placed between two plates. Bonus: one process per philosopher, and the forks are a pile in the middle, counted by a semaphore.

    

    

      
What you see

      
The program really runs: the table is drawn from what it prints (“200 3 is eating”). In the mandatory part, forks move to the philosopher who takes them, in the order the code uses to avoid deadlock. In the bonus, they come from the central pile.

    

    

      
Under the hood

      
The C code is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in a Web Worker. Pthreads become cooperative fibers there: each usleep(), mutex or semaphore hands over to the next one, on the real clock. For the bonus, fork() starts a “process” of fibers that kill() stops; named semaphores are emulated.
